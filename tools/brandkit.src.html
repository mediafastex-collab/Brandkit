The empty area around the logo that nothing else may enter.

x is the width of the chevron stroke. Keep at least 1x clear on every side of the mark, and 1.5x around lockups. No text, edges or other logos inside this area.

Voice & Tone

How we sound

Our voice is our personality in words and it stays the same everywhere. The tone shifts with the context: a cold email and a client report sound like the same person in different rooms.

Brand voiceMarketing toneWriting style

Personality

If Fastex Media were a person.

The operator in the room.

Calm, specific and sure of the system. Has the numbers when everyone else has opinions, and respects what the client has built.

Five voice traits

Every piece of copy should show all five.

Direct

Lead with the outcome. Give the number, the timeline and the next step. Cut anything a busy founder would skip.

Sounds like"We book meetings with plant heads. Worth 15 minutes on Thursday?"

Proof-first

Every claim travels with a figure, a client type or a method. If we can't prove it, we don't say it.

Sounds like"60% lower cost per lead, averaged across client accounts."

Calm confidence

Certainty comes from the system, not from adjectives. No hype, no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary".

Sounds like"Three moves. One machine."

Industry-fluent

Use the buyer's own words: EPC, OEM, admissions cycle, channel partners. Show we understand their market before we pitch.

Sounds like"Admissions teams don't need more enquiries in May. They need them in January."

Human

Respect what the client has built. Write like a sharp colleague, in plain English, with short sentences.

Sounds like"You've put a lot into building your business. Let's help more people discover it."

Context

Quick rules

What stays fixed whatever the context.

Never shout

No exclamation marks, all-caps sentences or "AMAZING". Ads get bolder through fewer words, not louder ones.

Always one next step

Every message ends with a single clear action: a call, a reply or a link. Never three.

Bad news first

When something goes wrong, say what happened before anything else, then the fix and the date.

Style rules

Small rules that keep every page consistent.

Name

Always Fastex Media: two words, capital F and M. "Fastex" is fine on second mention. Never FastEx, FASTEX MEDIA in running text, Fastex media or FM.

Numbers

Always use numerals: 3 channels, 15 minutes. Multipliers take a capital X with no space (3X). Percentages take the symbol (60%). Currency comes first: ₹50,000 or $2,500.

Headlines

Sentence case, 8 words or fewer and no full stop, unless it's one of the short punchy lines like "Three moves. One machine."

Spelling and dates

US English spelling (optimize, color) in public copy. Write dates as 4 Oct 2026. Use the Oxford comma. Use emoji only on Instagram and WhatsApp, and never more than one.

Design

How we look

Mostly paper and ink, with orange used sparingly so it stays a signal. Two typefaces and one angle, taken from the mark.

ColourTypographyGraphic language

Palette

Five colours. Click any value to copy it.

Proportion

On any one layout, orange should cover less than about a tenth of the area.

Paper & white 58%
Ink 30%
Orange 8%
4%

If everything is orange, nothing stands out.

Contrast

Measured against WCAG 2.1. AA is the minimum for any text.

Do

  • Use Ink text on Fastex Orange for buttons and badges (6.6:1).
  • Use Ember for links and small accent text on light backgrounds.
  • Use Fastex Orange for text only on Ink, or at 24 px and above in the logo and graphics.

Don't

  • Set white text on Fastex Orange. At 2.9:1 it fails accessibility.
  • Set orange body text on paper or white backgrounds (2.8:1).
  • Add gradients, tints or new hues to the logo.

Rules

How to set type so it always looks like us.

Tight on top, open below

Track headlines −1.5% to −3% and set body text at 1.6 line height. Keep body lines to about 65 characters.

Stats are the hero

Set numbers in Outfit SemiBold or Bold, tabular, large and in orange on Ink. Put the label underneath in Inter.

Fallbacks

If Outfit and Inter can't load (in Word, Gmail or PowerPoint), use Arial. Never use Times or Comic Sans, and never mix in a third typeface.

The 56° cut

Every diagonal in the mark leans 2 units across for every 3 down. Use that same angle everywhere.

56.3°The Fastex cut: 2 across, 3 down

Use it for image crops, colour bands, section dividers and motion paths. Never use 45°.

In CSS: transform: skewX(33.69deg). In Figma or Illustrator: rotate 33.69° from vertical.

Icons

2 px line icons on a 24 px grid with rounded caps, in Ink. Use Ember for at most one icon per set.

Imagery

Real work, real people, real evidence.

Use

  • Real people at work: sales teams, founders, site visits, factory floors and campuses from our five industries.
  • Real product evidence: dashboards, calendars and reply screens with private data blurred.
  • Warm, natural light. Darken images with a 60 to 75% Ink overlay when text sits on top.
  • Crops along the 56° cut.

Avoid

  • Stock handshakes, people pointing at screens, glowing brains and rocket ships.
  • Images that are recoloured orange. Orange belongs to graphics, not photos.
  • Busy backgrounds behind the logo.
  • Images of results that we can't back up.

Layout grid

Spacing, columns and corners.

8 px spacing

All spacing comes in steps of 8 px: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96. On social, use 6 to 9% of the width as the outer margin.

12 columns

Web uses 12 columns with a 24 px gutter and a 1200 px max width. Decks use the same grid at 1920 × 1080.

Corners

Buttons and tags are pills. Cards use 16 px corners and photos use 12 px. Don't mix corner styles on one surface.

Claims and compliance

What we can and can't say in paid media.

Do

  • Call every figure an average and say what it's an average of: "3X average lead growth across client accounts".
  • Keep a source sheet for every number used in an ad.
  • Get written approval before naming a client or using their logo.
  • Only use WhatsApp ads that click to chat with people who have opted in.

Don't

  • Say "guaranteed leads", "guaranteed ROI" or give a fixed meeting count without contract terms behind it.
  • Use competitor names or trademarks in ad copy or keywords without legal review.
  • Fake urgency: "only 2 spots left" unless it's true.
  • Put more than about 20% text on paid images. They reach fewer people.
